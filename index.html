The frontier, in the classroom —
while it's still the frontier.

Cultigen is an AI platform that helps educators — from K-12 to universities — keep their curricula on the frontier: diagnosing what to teach, coaching faculty to teach it, and turning the latest research into lessons, interactives, and buildable kits.

What Cultigen does ↓ Partner or invest →

Curricula lag for two reasons —
and neither is a lack of ideas.

The first is cost. Reviewing, revising, and re-approving courses across departments takes committees, semesters, and budgets — so a program only refreshes every few years. The constraint was never authorship; it was amortization.

The second is craft. Designing a good course — its pacing, scaffolding, and assessment — is a discipline in its own right, yet most faculty were never trained in it. For generations it's been tacit, experiential know-how, picked up on the job. Even a professor eager to modernize often lacks the method and the time to do it well.

So what students learn lags the frontier by years — from a middle-school science class to a graduate seminar — and drifts out of step with what the workforce needs. And when a course finally does modernize, the hands-on kits that make it stick are expensive and hard to customize. Cultigen attacks both: it guides faculty through course design and accelerates every step.

"Course design has always been a craft, learned on the job — not a discipline every teacher was ever trained in."

AI breaks the cost structure.

When diagnosing a curriculum, drafting a course, coaching an instructor, or spec'ing a hands-on kit drops from committees and semesters to an engine and a few minutes, keeping education current stops being a luxury. It becomes the default.

Stable core.
Living edges.

A curriculum shouldn't be entirely fluid — the fundamentals are the fundamentals. So Cultigen builds around a stable, standards-aligned core, then wraps it in frontier modules that stay current as the research moves.

The core gives students solid ground. The frontier modules keep them in contact with what's happening right now — and because original researchers review each one, "up to date" never means "unvetted."

It also changes what an institution is buying. Not a textbook that slowly goes stale — a curriculum that stays current.

FunguyLab: proof that
the kits can be built.

FunguyLab is a bioprinter that uses light and AI to guide living fungi into designed forms — a hands-on kit students can build in class, learning across biology, engineering, and design at once.

It grew out of our research, published at SIGGRAPH — the top computer-graphics venue. It began as an early proof of concept, and is now being adopted by universities and community labs. It's the clearest evidence that Cultigen's hands-on kits aren't a slide — they ship.
