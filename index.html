The best of what we discover
almost never reaches you.

Every year, some of the smartest people alive push the frontier forward. Most of it stays locked in papers, processes, and products that never become something a curious person can actually learn from or build.

And what does get taught often lags years behind, in a form that doesn't stick. The gap between what we know and what we get to touch keeps growing.

Cultigen closes that gap. It takes the newest research, an industry process, or a real product, and turns it into a course you can learn and build right now, while it is still the frontier.

FunguyLab:
our first course brand.

FunguyLab is the first course to come out of Cultigen. It uses light and AI to grow living fungi into designed forms, a hands-on kit that teaches biology, engineering, and design at once, built from my own research published at SIGGRAPH.

Course quality is a bigger
problem than it looks.

When teaching drifts, the cost lands on real people: students, graduates, and the public that funds research.

Is a degree still worth it?

In the AI age, more and more people question the value of higher education. The answer isn't a smaller university. It's making what happens inside one undeniably good.

Big debt, uneven teaching

Students take on serious debt for degrees whose teaching quality still has enormous room to improve. If the price is real, the learning should be too.

Taught vs. actually needed

What schools teach and what the world needs have drifted apart. Graduates leave underprepared and talent goes to waste, while employers still can't fill the roles they have. A course in step with the frontier closes both gaps at once.

Research stuck at the frontier

The path from a new discovery to a teachable lesson is slow and costly, so students keep learning yesterday's material. Better tools shorten that path.

Knowledge that never arrives

Too much research stops at "published." Work that could reach and benefit people never becomes something students can learn from and build on.

None of this means schools are obsolete.

It means the quality of the course is the thing worth fixing, and the thing Cultigen is built to fix.

Why I'm building Cultigen.

In about a year, I taught myself from fashion design into engineering, from a first research job at Tsinghua to Brown, RISD, and MIT. None of it came from a normal path. It came from getting my hands on the real thing, early.

Then I did the same for others. I mentored students that everyone had written off, one who had sent a dozen unanswered emails looking for a research spot, into places like Zhejiang University. It taught me that a degree measures opportunity, not ability, and that the right guidance, and the right hands-on experience, is what actually changes a life.

I'm a researcher at MIT CSAIL, in human-computer interaction and digital fabrication, the exact craft behind Cultigen's interactives and kits. I've watched brilliant research stay locked in papers, and curious people stay locked out of it.

Cultigen is how I put the frontier into everyone's hands.
